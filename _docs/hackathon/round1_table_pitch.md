# Round 1: Table Pitch (5 Minutes, No Q&A)

**Format:** 3 judges come to your table. 5 minutes. No Q&A — they leave when time's up. Top 20 selected.
**Setting:** Intimate. Phone in your hand. Objects on the table. Eye contact, not a projector.
**Goal:** Be memorable enough in 5 minutes that judges advocate for you during deliberation.

**After Round 1:** Judges deliberate. They may visit your GitHub repo, your demo link, and discuss among 30 judges total. 20 → 10 finalists selected.

---

## Judging Criteria (Score What They See)

| Criterion                             | Weight           | How They Score It at the Table                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Technical Implementation & Complexity | **25 pts** | What you*tell* them about architecture. They won't read code during the pitch — say it out loud. |
| Innovation                            | **20 pts** | What feels*new* about this. Tangible, not aspirational.                                           |
| Functionality & Reliability           | **20 pts** | Does the live demo work? Smoothly? Gracefully on failure?                                           |
| Design & UX                           | **15 pts** | Does the app look polished? Could a child actually use it?                                          |
| Presentation                          | **10 pts** | Are you clear, confident, engaging? Do you use your time well?                                      |
| Impact & Relevance                    | **10 pts** | Is this solving a real problem? Does the market make sense?                                         |

---

## BEFORE JUDGES ARRIVE (Checklist)

- [ ] App is open, camera viewfinder loaded
- [ ] Phone on mobile hotspot (**NOT** venue WiFi)
- [ ] Objects arranged left-to-right in demo order: water bottle → earbuds → toy excavator
- [ ] Backup objects nearby: potted plant, Rubik's cube
- [ ] Pre-recorded video queued on a second phone (nuclear backup)
- [ ] Fallback cache enabled for all demo objects
- [ ] Laptop open to GitHub repo (in case judge asks to see code or you want to flash the architecture)
- [ ] Everyone has water, no one is mid-chew
- [ ] Designate: who presents, who holds the phone, who handles backup if something crashes

## THE PITCH (5 minutes, no Q&A)

### 0:00 – 0:30 | THE HOOK (30 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Presentation (10 pts), Impact & Relevance (10 pts)

> *[Make eye contact with judges. Speak conversationally — you're 3 feet away, not on a stage.]*
>
> "My daughter is two and a half. She already grabs my phone and demands YouTube. Every time I hand it over, I feel a pang of guilt — she's absorbing passive content when she should be exploring the world."
>
> *[Brief pause — let it land.]*
>
> "Every parent knows this feeling. So we built SnapLearn — an app that turns the phone from a guilt machine for parents and a brainrot distributor for children into a learning tool. In short, you can think of it as Pokémon Go, but for English vocabulary."
>
> "Let me show you how it works."

*[Pick up the phone. Point camera at the water bottle.]*

---

### 0:30 – 1:15 | CAPTURE 1: Water Bottle — Common (45 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Functionality & Reliability (20 pts), Design & UX (15 pts), Technical Implementation (25 pts)

> *[Point camera at water bottle, tap capture]*
>
> "I point the camera at this water bottle and snap."
>
> *[Anticipation animation plays — "What did you find...?"]*
>
> "In under five seconds, the AI identifies the object and returns it in Vietnamese and English — 'Chai nước, Water Bottle' — with a rarity tier. This is Common, worth 10 points."
>
> *[TTS plays automatically]*
>
> "The app speaks the word aloud in both languages. Audio-first — because our users are six years old and can't read yet. Every interaction is ears and taps, no text menus."
>
> *[Tap pronunciation button, say "water bottle" clearly]*
>
> "Now I say it back. The speech recognition checks my pronunciation. Three stars — collected."
>
> *[Show points awarded]*

---

### 1:15 – 2:15 | CAPTURE 2: Earbuds — Uncommon + Fail/Retry (60 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Functionality & Reliability (20 pts), Design & UX (15 pts), Innovation (20 pts)

> "Let's try something harder."
>
> *[Point at earbuds, snap]*
>
> "Earbuds — that's Uncommon. Green glow. Worth 25 points. See how the rarity system works? Common objects are everywhere. Rare ones motivate kids to explore — go to the market, the park, a museum."
>
> *[TTS plays]*
>
> *[Tap pronunciation button — deliberately mispronounce]*
>
> "Now watch what happens when the pronunciation isn't right."
>
> *[Show the retry prompt — encouraging mascot feedback in Vietnamese]*
>
> "No punishment. The mascot says 'Gần đúng rồi! Thử lại nha!' — 'Almost! Try again!' All audio, no text pop-ups. This is designed for six-year-olds building confidence, not adults cramming for an exam."
>
> *[Tap again, say "earbuds" correctly]*
>
> "Second try — three stars. The card is collected."

---

### 2:15 – 2:45 | CAPTURE 3: Toy Excavator — Epic (30 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Functionality & Reliability (20 pts), Design & UX (15 pts)

> "Now the fun part."
>
> *[Pick up toy excavator, hold it up for judges to see, then snap]*
>
> "A toy excavator — every kid loves construction vehicles."
>
> *[Wait for reveal — dramatic animation, purple or gold glow]*
>
> "Epic! One hundred points. That purple glow, that celebration animation — *that's* the moment that gets a child hooked on learning instead of TikTok."

---

### 2:45 – 3:00 | VAULT + LEADERBOARD (15 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Functionality & Reliability (20 pts), Design & UX (15 pts)

> *[Navigate to Words Vault]*
>
> "Every capture becomes a trophy card in their collection."
>
> *[Quick swipe through cards]*
>
> *[Navigate to Leaderboard]*
>
> "And they compete on a global leaderboard. Collection plus competition — that's what turns a learning app into a daily habit."

---

### 3:00 – 3:30 | WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT (30 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Innovation (20 pts), Design & UX (15 pts)

> *[Put the phone down. Make eye contact. This is the intellectual core.]*
>
> "Three things make SnapLearn different from every other kids' learning app."
>
> "**One: Passive to Active.** Every other kids' app is content the child consumes — videos, flashcards someone else made. SnapLearn makes the child the content creator. They go out into the world and build their own vocabulary. The real world is the curriculum."
>
> "**Two: Gamified Learning.** The same psychology that makes Pokémon Go addictive — rarity, collection, competition — channeled into vocabulary. Kids don't feel like they're studying. They feel like they're playing."
>
> "**Three: Audio-First UX.** Our user can't read. Every interaction is ears and taps. No text labels, no menus, no typing. This is genuinely accessible AI for pre-literate children."

---

### 3:30 – 4:05 | UNDER THE HOOD (35 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Technical Implementation & Complexity (25 pts), Functionality & Reliability (20 pts)

> *[Shift tone to technical confidence. These judges may be engineers.]*
>
> "Under the hood — three AI vision models: GPT, Gemini, and Claude, orchestrated through BAML with type-safe structured outputs and automatic failover. If one model is slow or down, the next picks up seamlessly. Zero glue code."
>
> "The full pipeline — camera snap to scored learning card — runs in under five seconds. Vision identification, text-to-speech in two languages, speech recognition, and scoring. All real-time, not a mockup."
>
> "Pronunciation scoring uses a two-stage approach: OpenAI ASR transcribes what the child says, then an LLM-as-judge evaluates the match against the target word. This gives us both low latency and nuanced scoring — it handles accent variations and partial matches gracefully. If the speech model understands you, a native speaker would too."
>
> "And when a child captures an object, we match it against their existing collection using hybrid semantic-keyword search — the same RAG technique used in enterprise search — so the app recognizes when you've found a new word versus a variation of one you already have."

**Delivery note:** This is the section that scores you 25 points on Technical Implementation. Don't rush it. Judges are listening for signals of real engineering depth. "BAML," "type-safe," "LLM-as-judge," "hybrid semantic-keyword search," "RAG" — these are the terms that signal Level 4.

---

### 4:05 – 4:20 | GOOGLE LENS DIFFERENTIATION (15 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Innovation (20 pts)

> "Now, the obvious comparison — Google Lens. Google Lens identifies an object and stops. Full stop. SnapLearn turns identification into a complete learning loop: hear the word, speak it back, get scored, collect it, compete. Lens is a dictionary. SnapLearn is a game that teaches."

---

### 4:20 – 4:45 | THE VISION (25 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Impact & Relevance (10 pts), Innovation (20 pts)

> "Today it's vocabulary. But the platform expands naturally."
>
> "Near term: the child's captured words become sentences, then personalized stories the AI generates and reads aloud. Teachers assign scavenger hunts — one teacher, thirty kids. Chinese language — the backend already returns trilingual data."
>
> "Longer term: the AI sees the same cat your child sees and asks: 'Is that your cat? What's its name?' The camera creates shared visual context — the AI becomes a companion, not a tool."
>
> "We're targeting a real market — eight million Vietnamese kids learning English, two to three billion dollars a year in tutoring spend."

---

### 4:45 – 5:00 | THE TEAM + CLOSE (15 seconds)

**Criteria addressed:** Presentation (10 pts), Impact & Relevance (10 pts)

> *[Slow down. Look the judges in the eyes.]*
>
> "We're four enterprise AI engineers. This hackathon, we built for the hardest user we've ever designed for — a six-year-old who can't read."
>
> *[Pause.]*
>
> "SnapLearn. So parents can hand over the phone and feel proud — not guilty."

*[Slight nod. Hold eye contact. Don't say "any questions" — there is no Q&A.]*

## TIMING CHECKPOINTS

Have a teammate give subtle hand signals at these marks:

| Time | You Should Be At...                | If Behind...                                                |
| ---- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1:00 | Starting Capture 2 (earbuds)       | Shorten Capture 1 narration                                 |
| 2:00 | Mid-Capture 2 (retry flow)         | Skip deliberate mispronunciation, just do one clean capture |
| 3:00 | Starting "Why This Is Different"   | Drop Vault/Leaderboard to 5 seconds                         |
| 4:00 | Starting "Google Lens" or "Vision" | Cut Google Lens section, merge into Vision                  |
| 4:30 | Starting "Team + Close"            | Go straight to closing tagline                              |

**If catastrophically behind (3:30+ and still demoing):** Skip to "Under the Hood" → Close. Technical depth + emotional close is the minimum viable pitch.

---

## EMERGENCY FALLBACKS

| Situation               | Action                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| App crashes             | Restart (< 5s). Continue talking: "While that reloads..." and cover the Innovation section verbally.                                                                          |
| API timeout (>8s)       | "The AI is thinking..." — if >12s, say "Let me show you one we captured earlier" and open Words Vault.                                                                       |
| WiFi + hotspot both die | Play pre-recorded video from second phone. Narrate over it.                                                                                                                   |
| ASR fails (noisy room)  | "It's noisy here — the speech recognition works best in normal environments, which is how kids actually use it. Let me show you the retry flow..."                           |
| Judges seem disengaged  | Skip directly to toy excavator. The Epic reveal animation always gets a reaction.                                                                                             |
| Object misidentified    | "The AI made an interesting call there — this is actually why we built the fallback chain. In the real product, the child re-snaps if it's wrong." Pivot to the next object. |

## TEAM ROLES DURING THE PITCH

| Role                    | Who                          | Responsibility                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Presenter**     | Product lead                 | Delivers the entire 5-minute pitch                                  |
| **Phone handler** | Can be presenter or teammate | Holds/operates the phone during demo                                |
| **Timer**         | Teammate                     | Subtle hand signals at checkpoints                                  |
| **Backup**        | Teammate                     | Second phone with pre-recorded video ready; laptop with GitHub open |

**Rule:** One voice during the pitch. Supporting teammates stand engaged but silent. If a judge makes eye contact with a teammate during the pitch, the teammate smiles and nods — doesn't start talking.

---

## WHAT JUDGES SEE AFTER ROUND 1 (Deliberation Prep)

After your 5 minutes, judges deliberate among themselves and may review:

- **GitHub repo** — Make sure README is polished, architecture is documented, commit history is real
- **Demo link** — Make sure the deployed app works independently (not just on your phone)
- **Each other's notes** — They'll describe your project in ~1 sentence. Make sure that sentence is: "The Pokémon Go for vocabulary app, built by the dad for his daughter"

### Optimize for the sentence they'll say about you:

> "It's the app where a dad built a camera thing for his kid to learn English — like Pokémon Go for vocabulary. The demo was smooth, the tech was legit — three AI models with failover, LLM-as-judge for pronunciation. And they've got a clear path to classrooms and storytelling."

That sentence contains: personal story + demo quality + technical credibility + believable vision. Your pitch delivers all four.
