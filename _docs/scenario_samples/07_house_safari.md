# Scenario 7 — "House Safari"

| Field               | Detail                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Character**       | Lan Anh, age 7, Grade 2                                                                     |
| **Target language** | English                                                                                     |
| **Profile**         | Level 6 Word Collector · 34 words captured · Collection Book unlocked at Level 5             |
| **Setting**         | Rainy Saturday afternoon, family apartment in Thu Duc, Ho Chi Minh City                      |

## Context

It is raining. Not the polite drizzle of a passing cloud but the full-throated downpour of a Ho Chi Minh City wet season Saturday, the kind that turns the street outside the apartment into a shallow river and makes the balcony railing hiss. Lan Anh has been lying on the sofa for twenty minutes watching the rain hit the window. She has read the same page of her picture book three times. Her older brother is at a friend's house. Mom is in the kitchen. The TV is off. The apartment — a typical three-bedroom unit on the sixth floor of a building in Thu Duc — feels very small and very boring.

She picks up mom's phone from the coffee table. She opens SnapLearn. Not because anyone told her to. Not because a notification appeared. Not because there is a mission waiting. She opens it because the camera viewfinder is the most interesting thing in the apartment right now — a window that turns ordinary objects into treasure.

The viewfinder fills the screen. Her rabbit avatar sits in the top-left corner beside the Level 6 badge. Point counter: 542 pts. Streak: 3 days. No daily mission is active — Lan Anh completed yesterday's and a new one will generate tomorrow. There is nothing telling her what to do. No guidance. No instruction. Just the camera, the apartment, and Saturday afternoon stretching out ahead of her.

What happens next is the purest expression of what SnapLearn was built for.

---

## The Living Room

Lan Anh sits up on the sofa and points the camera at the first thing she sees: the television. It is mounted on the wall across from her, dark and silent, reflecting the grey light from the window. She taps the capture button.

The sonar animation plays. The mascot squints at the image:

*"Hmm... ban tim thay gi the...?"*
("Hmm... what did you find...?")

The card slides up. White shimmer. Common.

**ti vi** — the Vietnamese label.

*"Day la ti vi. Tieng Anh la: television."*
("This is a television. In English: television.")

**television** · /ˈtel.ɪ.vɪʒ.ən/ · Common ⚪ · 10 pts

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment:** Lan Anh does not read the word "television." She hears it. Four syllables, unfamiliar, a little grand-sounding for a seven-year-old. The TTS pronounces it slowly and clearly. The phonetic guide on the card is there for reference, but the sound does the teaching.

She taps the PTT button. "Te-le-vi-sion," she says, splitting it into neat pieces. The ASR returns: "television." Exact match. ⭐⭐⭐. The card tucks itself into the Collection Book icon. The camera returns. Points: 552.

That was easy. What else is there?

Her eyes land on the remote control, wedged between the sofa cushions. She fishes it out, holds it up, and snaps.

The screen flashes **green**. A warm bell tone. The card rises with an emerald glow.

*"Oa! Tu khong pho bien day!"*
("Oh! That's an uncommon one!")

**dieu khien** · remote control · Uncommon 🟢 · **25 pts**

The mascot bounces with excitement. The green border pulses. Twenty-five points for something that has been hiding in the sofa cushions all along.

*"Day la dieu khien. Tieng Anh la: remote control."*

Lan Anh tries the pronunciation. "Re-mote con-trol." ASR returns: "remote control." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 577.

She is smiling now. She stands up on the sofa and looks around the living room from this new vantage point. On the wooden shelf beside the TV, between a framed family photo and a ceramic vase, she spots it: grandpa's bonsai tree. It has been there for as long as she can remember — a small juniper with a curved trunk, maybe thirty centimeters tall, sitting in a shallow brown pot. She has never thought about it. It is just a thing on the shelf. She climbs off the sofa, walks over, and takes a photograph.

The loading animation plays. The mascot squints. The dotted circle spins.

Then the screen **explodes in gold**.

A fanfare plays — not the gentle chime of Common or the warm bell of Uncommon, but a full, bright, unmistakable fanfare that fills the quiet apartment. Gold light pours from the edges of the card as it rises. The mascot's voice cracks with joy:

*"TROI OI! TU HUYEN THOAI! BAN TIM DUOC TU HUYEN THOAI!"*
("OH MY! A LEGENDARY WORD! YOU FOUND A LEGENDARY WORD!")

**cay canh** · bonsai · Legendary 🟡 · **250 pts**

The gold border pulses. Tiny animated sparkles rain upward from the card. A small badge appears: "Only 2% of explorers have captured this!" Lan Anh's mouth falls open. She stares at the card — her own photo of grandpa's bonsai, the one she has walked past every single day, now glowing gold on the screen.

*"Day la cay canh. Tieng Anh la: bonsai."*

> 💡 **Design principle — Rarity transforms the familiar.** The bonsai has been on that shelf for years. Lan Anh has never once thought of it as special. But the rarity system reframes it: because only 2% of SnapLearn users have captured a bonsai, it is Legendary. The object did not change. The child's perception of it did. This is the core emotional engine of the rarity mechanic (§2.2) — not just rewarding difficult finds, but teaching children to see their own world with new eyes. The boring shelf in the boring living room on the boring rainy Saturday just produced the rarest card Lan Anh has ever seen.

"Bo-n-sai," she says into the PTT button, carefully. ASR: "bonsai." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: **827**. She has nearly doubled her total score in three captures.

Lan Anh looks up from the phone. She looks at the living room — the sofa, the curtains, the bookshelf, the lamp, the fan on the ceiling, the clock on the wall. Every single object is a word. Every single word might be gold.

She starts running.

## The Kitchen

She rounds the corner into the kitchen at a half-sprint, socks sliding on the tile floor. Mom looks up from the counter where she is chopping vegetables.

"Con lam gi vay?" ("What are you doing?")

"Con dang bat tu!" ("I'm catching words!")

She raises the phone and photographs the refrigerator — the big white Samsung humming in the corner.

White shimmer. Common ⚪.

**tu lanh** · refrigerator · 10 pts

*"Day la tu lanh. Tieng Anh la: refrigerator."*

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping — §2.3 Category diversity:** This is Lan Anh's first Kitchen-category capture. The Collection Book now has a new category populating. The system tracks this silently — category breadth feeds into diversity bonuses and helps generate future missions tailored to her capture gaps.

"Ree-fri-juh-ray-tor." Four syllables, careful. ASR: "refrigerator." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 837.

She pulls open the refrigerator door. Inside, on the middle shelf, a carton of eggs. She grabs one, holds it up to the camera in her palm, and snaps.

Common ⚪.

**trung** · egg · 10 pts

*"Day la trung. Tieng Anh la: egg."*

"Egg!" One syllable. Easy. ⭐⭐⭐. She puts the egg back carefully. Points: 847.

She spins around. On the counter beside mom's cutting board, the rice cooker — a Sunhouse model with a dented lid, used twice a day every day, the most essential appliance in a Vietnamese kitchen. She photographs it.

Green glow. Uncommon 🟢.

**noi com dien** · rice cooker · 25 pts

*"Day la noi com dien. Tieng Anh la: rice cooker."*

The mascot's voice rises with approval. And then, a new notification slides in beneath the card — a subtle golden bar:

**Da dang bo suu tap! +10 XP**
("Collection diversity bonus! +10 XP")

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping — §2.2 Category diversity bonus in action:** Lan Anh has now captured words in two distinct categories within a single session — Living Room and Kitchen. The diversity bonus rewards breadth of exploration. Per §2.2: "The 5th animal captured is worth less than the 1st musical instrument — the system rewards breadth." She is hitting multiple rooms, multiple object types, and the system is noticing.

"Rice coo-ker." ASR: "rice cooker." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 882.

Mom watches from the cutting board. She sees Lan Anh photographing the rice cooker with the intensity of a wildlife photographer and smiles. She does not interrupt.

> 💡 **Design principle — The world is the curriculum (§2.1).** Lan Anh is not following a lesson plan. No one told her to go to the kitchen. No mission is active. She is running from room to room because the collection mechanic has transformed her apartment into a game board. The rice cooker, the eggs, the refrigerator — objects she sees every single day — are suddenly worth capturing, worth pronouncing, worth collecting. The intrinsic motivation is not "I should study English." It is "what's in the next room?"

## The Bedroom

Down the hallway. Past the bathroom (she will come back to that). Into her bedroom — the one she shares with her brother, with bunk beds against the wall and a desk under the window where the rain is still hammering.

She photographs her pillow first. It is lying on the lower bunk, slightly squished, with a faded Doraemon pillowcase.

Common ⚪.

**goi** · pillow · 10 pts

*"Day la goi. Tieng Anh la: pillow."*

"Pi-llow." ASR: "pillow." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 892.

The alarm clock on the bedside shelf — a small pink analog clock with two bells on top, a gift from an aunt, rarely used because mom wakes them up anyway.

Green glow. Uncommon 🟢.

**dong ho bao thuc** · alarm clock · 25 pts

*"Day la dong ho bao thuc. Tieng Anh la: alarm clock."*

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment:** "Alarm clock" is a compound noun — two words, two concepts. The TTS delivers it as a single unit, with natural stress on "a-LARM." Lan Anh hears the rhythm of the phrase before she sees the two separate words on the card. Audio teaches phrasing; text alone would not.

"A-larm clock." ASR: "alarm clock." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 917.

She opens the desk drawer. Pencils, erasers, stickers, a tangled charging cable — and a pair of scissors with orange plastic handles. She holds them up.

Common ⚪.

**keo** · scissors · 10 pts

*"Day la keo. Tieng Anh la: scissors."*

"Si-zors." ASR: "scissors." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 927. A new category tag appears on the card: School/Stationery. Another category. Another tick on the diversity tracker.

Three rooms. Nine words. Lan Anh has been at this for maybe eight minutes. She is not tired. She is not bored. The boredom that started this session is a distant memory, replaced by the momentum of rapid-fire captures — camera, snap, card, pronounce, next. The rhythm is fast and addictive. Every card is a small reward. Every room is a new zone.

## The Bathroom

She pushes open the bathroom door. The light is already on — someone left it on. The room is small: a toilet, a sink, a mirror above the sink, a shelf with toothbrushes and soap.

She raises the camera to photograph the mirror. And then she sees herself.

Her own face stares back from the phone screen — the camera pointed at the mirror, reflecting the phone reflecting the camera reflecting the phone. She is in the photo. Big eyes, messy ponytail, a grin she did not know she was wearing. She giggles. She takes the photo anyway.

Common ⚪.

**guong** · mirror · 10 pts

*"Day la guong. Tieng Anh la: mirror."*

> 💡 **Design principle — Joy is the product.** This moment — a seven-year-old laughing at her own face in a mirror because she accidentally photographed herself while trying to capture a vocabulary word — is not an edge case. It is the point. SnapLearn does not work because of its AI pipeline or its rarity algorithm or its spaced repetition framework. It works because it gives children a reason to run through the house, point a camera at things, and laugh. The learning is real. The pronunciation practice is pedagogically sound. But the joy comes first. Everything else follows.

"Mi-rror." ASR: "mirror." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 937.

The toothbrush. Her own, the blue one, third from the left in the cup on the shelf.

Common ⚪.

**ban chai danh rang** · toothbrush · 10 pts

*"Day la ban chai danh rang. Tieng Anh la: toothbrush."*

Another compound noun — "tooth" plus "brush." The TTS links them into a single flowing word. "Tooth-brush." Lan Anh repeats: "Tooth-brush." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 947.

The diversity bonus notification appears again:

**Da dang bo suu tap! +10 XP**

Four distinct categories now. Living Room, Kitchen, Bedroom, Bathroom. The system rewards her not for volume — eleven Common captures would yield only 110 points — but for breadth. She is mapping her entire apartment.

## The Balcony

The balcony is narrow — just wide enough for a drying rack, a few potted plants, and a pair of plastic chairs that grandma uses when she visits. The rain is still falling hard, but the balcony is covered, and Lan Anh steps out onto the cool tile in her socks. The air smells like wet concrete and jasmine from the neighbor's pot below.

She photographs her slippers — the pink rubber ones with a flower pattern, sitting by the balcony door.

Common ⚪.

**dep** · slippers · 10 pts

*"Day la dep. Tieng Anh la: slippers."*

"Sli-ppers." ASR: "slippers." ⭐⭐⭐. Points: 957.

She turns to go back inside. And then she sees it.

On the top shelf of the old wooden bookcase that grandma moved here from her house in the countryside — the bookcase that holds extra blankets and photo albums and things no one looks at — there is something Lan Anh has seen a thousand times without ever really seeing it. A wooden frame. Rows of beads on metal rods. Dark wood, worn smooth at the edges. Grandma's abacus. It has been on that shelf since before Lan Anh was born.

She reaches up on her toes, carefully takes it down, and photographs it on the plastic chair.

The screen flashes **purple**.

An orchestral swell — deep, rich, resonant, completely different from every other sound the app has made today. The card rises with a violet aurora shimmering around its edges, light rippling like the surface of water. The mascot's voice is astonished:

*"Khong the tin duoc! Tu cuc hiem! Do la tu Epic!"*
("Unbelievable! Super rare! That's an Epic word!")

**ban tinh** · abacus · Epic 🟣 · **100 pts**

The purple aura pulses. The mascot does a slow, reverent spin. A hundred points. The second-highest rarity Lan Anh has ever seen, topped only by the Legendary bonsai from twenty minutes ago.

*"Day la ban tinh. Tieng Anh la: abacus."*

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment:** "Abacus" is not a word a seven-year-old Vietnamese child has ever heard in English. It is not in her Grade 2 textbook. It is not in any textbook she will encounter until much later. But she is hearing it now — clearly, correctly, in context — while holding the actual object in her hand. The TTS says /ˈæb.ə.kəs/, three syllables, and Lan Anh repeats it with the wooden beads still warm from her palm.

"A-ba-cus." The ASR processes. Returns: "abacus." ⭐⭐⭐.

Points: **1,057**. She has broken a thousand.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping — §2.2 Dynamic rarity determination:** The abacus is Epic because almost no children photograph one. It is an object from a previous generation — grandma's tool, not Lan Anh's. But it was sitting on a shelf in her own apartment, and she walked past it every day. Rarity is not about difficulty of access. It is about probability of capture. The system rewards the child who notices the thing everyone else overlooks.

Lan Anh puts the abacus back on the shelf. She holds the phone to her chest. It is still raining. The apartment is still the same apartment. But it does not feel boring anymore.

## The Collection Book Check

She taps the 📖 icon. The Collection Book opens.

Nine minutes ago, this book had 34 words scattered across a few categories. Now it has 48. The new captures fill in across the category grid:

| Category | Before | After | New words added |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Living Room / Home | 8 | 11 | television, remote control, bonsai |
| Kitchen | 0 | 3 | refrigerator, egg, rice cooker |
| Bedroom | 3 | 6 | pillow, alarm clock, scissors |
| Bathroom | 0 | 2 | mirror, toothbrush |
| Clothing / Accessories | 2 | 3 | slippers |
| Tools / Objects | 1 | 2 | abacus |

Two entirely new categories — Kitchen and Bathroom — have opened from zero. She can see the empty slots in each category, the grey silhouettes with question marks, hinting at words she has not yet found. There is a whisk in the kitchen. A soap dispenser in the bathroom. A curtain in the bedroom. The empty slots are not a rebuke — they are an invitation.

The mascot narrates as she browses:

*"Bo suu tap cua ban dang lon len! Ban da kham pha nhieu phong lam!"*
("Your collection is growing! You've explored so many rooms!")

> 💡 **Design principle — The Collection Book as spatial map of the child's world.** The category pages are not abstract taxonomies. They are rooms. They are places. The Kitchen page is *her* kitchen — refrigerator, egg, rice cooker, all photographed by her, all showing her photos. The Collection Book becomes a map of Lan Anh's apartment, built by Lan Anh, one capture at a time. When she visits grandma's house next weekend, the book will grow to include grandma's kitchen, grandma's garden, grandma's living room. The map expands as the child's world expands. The world is the curriculum, and the Collection Book is the atlas.

She closes the book. The camera returns.

The rain has not stopped. She does not care.

## What Just Happened

Fourteen words in fifteen minutes. No mission. No leaderboard check. No teacher. No parent prompting. Just a bored seven-year-old on a rainy Saturday who picked up a phone and discovered that her own apartment — the most familiar, most ordinary place she knows — is full of words she has never learned, in a language she is just beginning to understand. She ran from room to room. She laughed at her own face in the mirror. She held grandma's abacus for the first time. She learned that the thing on the shelf is called a "bonsai" and that almost nobody else has captured one.

She learned fourteen English words. She does not know this. What she knows is that she found a gold card and a purple card and her points went past a thousand and the Collection Book is filling up and there are still words in the kitchen she has not captured yet and maybe tomorrow she will go to grandma's house and find even more.

The studying happened. She did not notice.

## Product & Technical Notes

### No Mission Required
This session had no active mission. Lan Anh completed yesterday's daily mission and a new one had not yet generated. The entire session was driven by intrinsic curiosity and the collection mechanic alone. This is a critical design validation: if the core loop only works when missions are active, the product is fragile. Lan Anh's house safari demonstrates that the capture-collect-earn loop is self-sustaining. Missions are accelerants, not fuel.

### Category Diversity Bonus Mechanics
The diversity bonus triggered twice during this session — once after the third distinct category (Kitchen, following Living Room and Bedroom) and once after the fourth (Bathroom). The bonus is a flat +10 XP per new category within a single session, capped at 5 bonuses per session (50 XP maximum). This reward is modest but visible, and it explicitly encourages the behavior SnapLearn most values: exploring broadly rather than capturing deeply in a single domain.

### Rarity Distribution in the Home
Most household objects are Common, and that is by design. The volume of Common captures creates forward momentum — quick captures, fast rhythm, points accumulating steadily. The Uncommon and higher-rarity finds punctuate that rhythm with spikes of excitement. In this session, the ratio was 9 Common : 3 Uncommon : 1 Epic : 1 Legendary. The Legendary bonsai and Epic abacus were not planted by the system — they were real objects in a real apartment that the dynamic rarity algorithm elevated because so few users ever capture them. The home contains more surprise than anyone expects.

### Physical Movement
Lan Anh visited five distinct areas of her apartment in fifteen minutes. She stood up from the sofa, ran to the kitchen, walked down the hallway, entered the bathroom, and stepped onto the balcony. SnapLearn does not track steps or require physical movement, but the capture mechanic naturally creates it: the next word is always in a different place. The app gets children off the sofa and moving through physical space without ever framing itself as exercise. On a rainy day when outdoor play is impossible, a house safari is the next best thing.

### Parent Dashboard View
That evening, mom opens the parent dashboard. The activity feed shows fourteen captures scrolling down the screen — each with Lan Anh's photo, the Vietnamese and English labels, rarity badges, and pronunciation scores. The rarity distribution chart has changed dramatically: a gold dot and a purple dot now sit among the white and green. The category diversity radar has expanded from a narrow wedge into something closer to a circle. A push notification arrived mid-session: "Lan Anh just found a Legendary word: bonsai!" Mom did not need the notification. She heard the fanfare from the kitchen.
