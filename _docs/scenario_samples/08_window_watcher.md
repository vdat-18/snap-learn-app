# Scenario 8 — "Window Watcher"

## Metadata

| Field                | Detail                                                                          |

| **Profile**          | Level 8 Word Collector · 48 words captured · 🔥 3-day streak                   |
| **Target language**  | English                                                                         |

---

## Context

Every Vietnamese child knows the Hồ Chí Minh City-to-Vũng Tàu drive. Two hours on the expressway — sometimes three with traffic — through an arc of landscape that starts in dense city, thins into highway greenery, and ends at the coast. For the adults, it is a scenic trip. For an eight-year-old in the back seat, it is a sentence. Phúc has exhausted his interest in the passing concrete before they've even cleared District 2. His sister Thi is in the front seat with headphones on. Mom is driving. The air conditioning hums. The world drifts by.

Phúc's phone sits on the seat beside him. SnapLearn is not the first thing he thinks of. The first thing he thinks of is boredom. The second thing is the phone. The third thing — the one that changes the next two hours — is a motorcycle weaving through traffic outside his window, close enough to touch.

---

## The Session

### City Streets

The motorcycle leans into view as Mom slows for a red light on Xa lộ Hà Nội. It is red and silver, its rider wearing a green helmet, a plastic bag of takeaway containers hanging from one handlebar. Phúc has seen ten thousand motorcycles in his life. He reaches for the phone anyway.

He opens SnapLearn. The camera viewfinder fills the screen — the car's headrest, his own knee, the window glass. He tilts the phone toward the window. The motorcycle is right there, one lane over, the rider scrolling his own phone at the red light. Phúc frames it through the glass and taps the capture button.

The screen freezes on the photo. The mascot appears, squinting:

*"Hmm... bạn tìm thấy gì thế...?"*
("Hmm... what did you find...?")

A beat. The card slides up from the bottom of the screen. White shimmer. Common ⚪.

**xe máy** — the Vietnamese label.

*"Đây là xe máy. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a motorbike. In English:")

**"Motorcycle."** `/ˈmoʊtərsaɪkəl/`

Common ⚪ · 10 pts. In a country where motorcycles outnumber people in some neighborhoods, this is the most pedestrian capture imaginable. Phúc does not care. He is not in the kitchen. He is not at school. He is in a moving car, and the camera just worked through the window. The glass, the angle, the distance — none of it mattered. The AI saw what he saw.

That changes everything.

He sits up straighter. He presses his face closer to the window. The city is suddenly full of things.

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment** — Phúc is looking out the car window, not at the phone screen. The TTS scaffolding plays the Vietnamese name, the bridge phrase, and the English word aloud. He absorbs the capture result through his ears while his eyes stay on the road, already scanning for the next target. In a car, the audio-first design is not a nice-to-have — it is the interface. The child's attention is split between the window and the phone, and audio bridges the gap. He hears "motorcycle" without ever reading it on the card.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping** — This is §2.1 Collection Loop operating in transit: spot → capture → reveal → hunt again. The loop is identical to the kitchen or the market, but the environment is moving. The collection mechanic does not require the child to be stationary. The world moves past, and the game moves with it.

The light turns green. Mom accelerates. Traffic thickens and slows again two hundred meters later — a construction bottleneck near the Cát Lái junction. Cars and trucks idle in a hot, frustrated queue. To Phúc, the traffic jam is not an inconvenience. It is a target-rich environment.

A truck sits in the next lane, massive, painted blue and orange, its flatbed loaded with steel rebar. The cab is level with Phúc's window. He can see the driver wiping his forehead with a towel. Phúc raises the phone. Tap.

Green glow. A warm, bell-like tone. Uncommon 🟢.

*"Đây là xe tải. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a truck. In English:")

**"Truck."** `/trʌk/`

Uncommon 🟢 · 25 pts. Trucks are large and visible but less frequently captured than household objects. The green border glows on the card. Phúc grins. Twenty-five points for sitting in traffic. The construction jam has become a gift.

*"Nào, nói thử đi!"*
("Now, try saying it!")

He presses the PTT button. "Truck!" he says, the /tr/ landing clean. ASR returns: `"truck"`. Exact match. ⭐⭐⭐.

> 💡 **Design principle — Every environment is a classroom.** The product vision's core premise (§2.1) is that the world is the curriculum. The car window literalizes this — the curriculum rolls past at sixty kilometers per hour. A traffic jam becomes a vocabulary mine. A highway becomes a conveyor belt of new categories. The app does not need to know the child is in a car. The collection loop works anywhere there are recognizable objects, and the real world never runs out of those.

Traffic loosens. Mom merges onto the expressway. Speed builds. The buildings thin. And Phúc spots it — a city bus in the express lane, green and white, its route number painted on the side in huge digits. He swings the phone toward it and taps capture.

This time the card does not appear.

The mascot returns to the screen, but its expression is different — head tilted, one paw scratching behind its ear, eyes narrowed with gentle confusion. The voice is warm, not disappointed:

*"Hmm, mình chưa rõ lắm — thử lại nhé!"*
("Hmm, I couldn't quite see — try again!")

The photo flashes briefly on screen: the bus is there, but smeared. The green-and-white paint is a long horizontal streak. The route number is illegible. Phúc was panning to follow the bus as it passed, and the combination of two vehicles moving in opposite-ish directions at highway speed produced a photo that the AI could not parse with confidence. The confidence score came back at 0.58 — below the 0.7 threshold.

The camera viewfinder returns. No penalty. No lost points. No scolding. Just the invitation to try again.

Phúc watches the bus pull ahead. It is moving faster than Mom's car. It shrinks. He thinks the moment is gone. But two minutes later, the bus is stopped at the expressway toll station, and Mom's car pulls up three lanes over. Phúc can see it clearly — stationary, sharp, the green-and-white livery bright in the morning sun.

He taps capture.

*"Hmm... bạn tìm thấy gì thế...?"*

White shimmer. Common ⚪.

*"Đây là xe buýt. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a bus. In English:")

**"Bus."** `/bʌs/`

Common ⚪ · 10 pts. The card shows a crisp photograph of the bus at rest, clear as a textbook illustration. Confidence: 0.94. Phúc pumps his fist. The bus that escaped is now in his collection.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping** — This is §3.1 Vision: "Low confidence (<0.7) triggers a retry: 'Hmm, I'm not sure what that is — try getting closer!'" The retry flow is designed to be encouraging, not punishing. The mascot's tone is curious, not critical. The phrasing — "thử lại nhé" ("try again!") — is an invitation, not a correction. In a moving-vehicle scenario, "getting closer" may not be possible, but the child can wait for the environment to deliver a second chance. The toll station provided that chance organically. The retry arc — failed capture, patience, natural re-encounter, success — is more satisfying than a first-try capture would have been.

> 💡 **Design principle — Failure as narrative, not punishment.** The blurry bus photo is not a bug in the car scenario. It is the scenario. The motion blur creates a challenge — snap it before it passes! — and the retry creates a story: "I missed the bus, but then I caught it at the toll." The app does not penalize Phúc for the blur. It does not subtract points or streak progress. It simply invites him to try again. In a car, where the child cannot control speed or proximity, this grace is essential. The game adapts to the constraint.

### The Highway

Past the toll station, the expressway opens. Six lanes of smooth asphalt, the city receding in the mirrors. The landscape shifts. Buildings give way to warehouses, then to open fields, then to green. The air outside, if Phúc could smell it, would change from exhaust to grass. Through the window, the palette transforms — grey concrete replaced by the deep green of rubber plantations and rice paddies stretching to distant treelines.

Phúc notices before he understands. The things outside the window are different. The things are new.

A row of trees lines the highway median — tall, thin trunks with spreading canopies. Phúc frames one through the window. It is not a perfect photograph — the tree is slightly blurred, the car doing ninety — but the canopy is distinct, the trunk is recognizable, and the sky behind provides clean contrast. Tap.

White shimmer. Common ⚪.

*"Đây là cây. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a tree. In English:")

**"Tree."** `/triː/`

Common ⚪ · 10 pts. The most basic nature word — every child in Vietnam can point to a tree. But Phúc's Collection Book tells a different story. He has forty-eight words, and until this moment, every single one of them was from the city: household items, school supplies, food, vehicles. `cây` is his first word in the Nature category. The app notices.

A small banner slides in below the card, green and leafy:

*"Chủ đề mới: Thiên nhiên! Tiếp tục khám phá nhé!"*
("New category: Nature! Keep exploring!")

A category diversity bonus adds 5 extra XP. It is small, but the visual — the leafy banner, the new category icon appearing in Phúc's profile — signals that something has shifted. The world outside the car window is not the same world as the kitchen. New categories live out here.

> 💡 **Design principle — Category diversity as natural curriculum expansion (§2.1, §2.2).** Phúc did not choose to study Nature vocabulary. The highway chose for him. As the landscape changes, the available captures change, and new categories unlock organically. The app's category diversity bonus gently rewards this breadth — a child who captures across many categories earns more than one who repeats a single domain. In the car scenario, diversity is effortless: the city gives vehicles, the highway gives nature, the coast will give something else entirely. The curriculum is the road.

A bridge appears ahead — the Long Thành bridge, maybe, or one of the nameless overpasses that stitch the expressway together. Concrete pillars rising from marshy ground, the road lifting and then descending. Phúc frames the bridge through the windshield as they approach.

*"Đây là cầu. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a bridge. In English:")

**"Bridge."** `/brɪdʒ/`

Uncommon 🟢 · 25 pts. The green glow pulses on the card. Bridges are visible but infrequent — you do not encounter one in your kitchen or your classroom. This is highway vocabulary. The points counter ticks upward. The Nature category gains another entry.

"Nào, nói thử đi!"

Phúc holds PTT. "Bridge!" — the /dʒ/ is slightly soft, more like "britch," but the ASR returns `"bridge"`. Match. ⭐⭐⭐.

Mom glances in the rearview mirror. Phúc is hunched toward the window, phone raised, eyes darting across the landscape like a hawk scanning a field. She has not heard a single "Mẹ ơi, khi nào mình tới?" ("Mom, are we there yet?") since they left the city. She does not know exactly what he is doing, but she knows silence from the back seat on the Vũng Tàu highway is a small miracle. She drives.

Then Phúc's sister speaks.

"Look," Thi says from the front seat, pulling one earbud out. She is pointing out the right window at a field just off the highway. "Bò kìa!" ("Cows!")

Phúc whips the phone to the right. Through the window — a cluster of brown and white cows standing in ankle-deep grass, some grazing, some staring at the expressway with the empty patience of animals who have never been anywhere else. The car is doing eighty. The cows are shrinking. Phúc has maybe three seconds.

He taps capture.

The screen freezes. The mascot squints. Phúc holds his breath.

White shimmer. Common ⚪.

*"Đây là bò. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a cow. In English:")

**"Cow."** `/kaʊ/`

The confidence came back at 0.73 — just above the threshold. The photo is not crisp. The cows are small in the frame, slightly elongated by motion. But the AI identified the dominant subject: brown and white bovine shapes in a green field. Good enough. In the card, the photo has a certain accidental charm — three cows caught mid-graze through a car window, a little blurry, a little impressionistic, like a painting you would find in a café.

Common ⚪ · 10 pts. But the category banner slides in again:

*"Chủ đề mới: Động vật!"*
("New category: Animals!")

Another diversity bonus. Phúc has now opened three categories in a single car ride: Vehicles, Nature, Animals. The highway is a category factory.

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment** — The cow capture is pure reflex. Thi spotted the cows, called out, and Phúc reacted — point, tap, done. He did not look at the screen during the capture. He looked at the cows. The TTS result played in his ear while his eyes were already scanning the next field: *"Đây là bò. Tiếng Anh là: cow."* The audio-first design means the capture-reveal loop can run almost entirely through sound while the child's visual attention stays on the world outside the window. In a car, where the scenery is fleeting and the child's gaze is split between phone and window, this is not convenience — it is necessity.

Thi puts her earbud back in. But five minutes later, she pulls it out again. "Phúc, mây kìa!" ("Phúc, clouds!") She is pointing at the sky through the sunroof. High cumulus clouds, fat and bright, stacking against the blue like a child's drawing. Thi has been drawn into the game without downloading the app.

Phúc tilts the phone upward, angling it against the window glass, pointing at the sky. The viewfinder shows mostly sky — a wash of blue with white cloud billowing across the upper third. It is not a conventional photo of an object. It is sky. He taps capture.

The AI processes. The image is mostly blue gradient with a white irregular mass. There is no household object, no animal, no vehicle. But the multimodal LLM has seen ten million photographs of clouds, and it knows what cumulus looks like from below.

White shimmer. Common ⚪.

*"Đây là đám mây. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a cloud. In English:")

**"Cloud."** `/klaʊd/`

Common ⚪ · 10 pts. The card shows Phúc's photograph: a rectangle of blue sky with a bright white cloud formation dominating the frame. It looks like an art photograph. It is his best-looking card.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping** — This is §3.1 Vision handling a non-standard composition. The AI does not require a centered, well-lit object on a clean background. Modern multimodal LLMs can identify the dominant visual element in landscape shots, sky photos, distance shots, and angled perspectives. A photo that is "mostly sky with a cloud" is correctly parsed as `cloud` with high confidence (0.91 in this case). The system is robust to the creative angles that naturally occur when a child photographs the world through a car window.

> 💡 **Design principle — Family participation as invisible onboarding.** Thi — who does not use SnapLearn, who was listening to music, who is thirteen and too old for vocabulary games — just spotted two captures for her little brother. The car scenario naturally draws family members in. Mom drives and provides the vehicle. Thi calls out targets. Phúc operates the camera. The app is single-player in design but multiplayer in practice. No feature was built for this. The collection loop is simply compelling enough that bystanders start pointing things out.

### The Coast

The highway descends toward Bà Rịa. The air changes — Phúc cannot feel it through the car's air conditioning, but something shifts in the light. Warmer. Brighter. The green of inland plantations gives way to scrubby coastal vegetation and roadside stalls selling dried squid and inflatable beach toys. The road curves, and through a gap between two low buildings, Phúc sees it.

The sea.

It appears and disappears between buildings for the next five minutes — glimpses of flat blue, then concrete, then blue again — until the road crests a hill and the ocean opens up in a wide panorama. Bãi Sau beach. The water is grey-green under the midday sun, stretching to the horizon, and for a moment Phúc just stares at it. He has been to Vũng Tàu before, but the first glimpse of the sea from the car always stops him.

Then he raises the phone.

He frames the ocean through the window — the water filling the lower two-thirds of the screen, the sky above, a thin strip of beach visible at the edge. Tap.

Green glow. Warm bell tone. Uncommon 🟢.

*"Đây là biển. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is the sea. In English:")

**"Sea."** `/siː/`

Uncommon 🟢 · 25 pts. The card glows green. Phúc's photograph of the ocean is wide and flat and beautiful — the kind of image that looks intentional even though it was shot one-handed through a car window at seventy kilometers an hour. The word `biển` sits above `sea` on the card, and the TTS says both, and the sound of the English word is so simple — one syllable, one clean vowel — that it feels like the ocean named itself.

"Sea!" Phúc repeats without being prompted, before the "Say it!" button even appears. He is already learning. The PTT prompt appears and he presses it, says "sea" cleanly. ASR match. ⭐⭐⭐.

The coastal road follows the shoreline. Mom weaves through Vũng Tàu's narrow streets — scooters, fruit carts, tourists in sun hats. Through the window, Phúc spots fishing boats moored in the harbor — wooden hulls painted blue and red, tangled nets drying on the docks, the smell of salt and diesel somewhere beyond the glass.

He frames a boat. The largest one, with a high prow and faded paint. Tap.

Green glow again. Uncommon 🟢.

*"Đây là thuyền. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a boat. In English:")

**"Boat."** `/boʊt/`

Uncommon 🟢 · 25 pts. A fishing boat in a coastal harbor — not something a child in District 1 sees during a school week. The rarity engine rewards the geography. Phúc's Nature category is building depth. Vehicles, Nature, Animals — and now the coast is adding maritime vocabulary that none of his classmates in the city will have captured today.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping** — This is §2.2 Rarity & Points: "Rarity is determined dynamically based on global capture frequency." A fishing boat in Vũng Tàu is Uncommon because most SnapLearn users are city-based schoolchildren who do not encounter harbors daily. If Phúc lived in a fishing village, boats might register as Common. The rarity engine is geographic and demographic — it rewards encounters that are unusual for the user population, not unusual in absolute terms. Travel naturally increases rarity potential because travel puts the child in unfamiliar environments.

Mom signals a turn. "We're stopping for a break," she says, pulling into a rest stop along the beachfront road. The car slows, crunches over gravel, and parks under a row of trees. Phúc's sister unbuckles and heads for the restroom. Mom stretches. Phúc opens his door and steps out into the heat and the wind and the blinding coastal sun.

He is no longer shooting through glass. He is outside, phone in hand, and the world is sharp.

The rest stop is a gravel lot with a small drink stall, plastic chairs, and a row of coconut palms between the parking area and the beach. The palms are tall — ten meters, maybe twelve — with curved trunks and heavy clusters of green coconuts hanging below the fronds. Phúc has seen coconut palms in pictures. He has drunk coconut water from street vendors in Saigon. But these are real and enormous and right in front of him.

He frames a palm from below, tilting the phone up so the trunk stretches diagonally across the screen, the coconut cluster hanging against the sky like a green chandelier. Tap.

The screen flashes. Not white. Not green.

Blue.

A deep, resonant chime fills the air — richer and heavier than the Common or Uncommon tones, a sound that vibrates in the chest. The card rises from the bottom of the screen with a steady blue pulse radiating from its edges, light rippling outward like rings on water.

**Rare 🔵.**

The mascot's voice shifts register — higher energy, breathless:

*"Oa! Bạn tìm được từ hiếm! Tuyệt vời!"*
("Wow! You found a rare word! Amazing!")

The card shows Phúc's photograph — the coconut palm from below, trunk curving, coconuts hanging, the sky a perfect blue backdrop. Below it:

**cây dừa** — the Vietnamese label.

*"Đây là cây dừa. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a coconut palm. In English:")

**"Coconut palm."** `/ˈkoʊkənʌt pɑːm/`

Rare 🔵 · **50 pts.** The blue glow continues to pulse. In the corner, the mascot does a backflip.

Fifty points. The reason is simple: most SnapLearn users are urban schoolchildren. They capture chairs and cups and backpacks. Coconut palms grow in coastal and rural Vietnam, not in apartment complexes in District 7. The global capture frequency for `cây dừa` is low. The rarity engine rewards Phúc for being somewhere unusual.

"Coconut palm!" Phúc says into the PTT button, carefully splitting the compound word. The ASR returns `"coconut palm"`. Match. ⭐⭐⭐. The blue card tucks itself into his Collection with a satisfied pulse.

> 💡 **Design principle — Travel as rarity multiplier.** The rest stop break demonstrates a key dynamic of the rarity system: leaving your home environment increases the probability of Rare and higher captures. A city child at the coast encounters objects that are common to coastal residents but rare in the global user base (which skews urban). This creates a natural incentive to capture during trips, vacations, and visits to unfamiliar places. The boring car ride is not dead time — it is transit to a rarity-rich zone. The app does not know Phúc is traveling. It does not need to. The rarity engine responds to what he photographs, and travel changes what he photographs.

Phúc is electric now. He scans the rest stop with the intensity of a jewel thief casing a museum. The drink stall — too common. The plastic chairs — definitely captured before. The gravel lot — not an object. But past the coconut palms, past the beach, out on the point where the coastline curves southward, he sees something.

A lighthouse.

It is maybe three hundred meters away, standing on a rocky promontory above the surf. White and cylindrical, with a red-capped lantern room at the top and a narrow balcony circling the upper section. It is not close. But it is unmistakable — the shape of a lighthouse is one of those universal silhouettes that even an eight-year-old recognizes instantly.

Phúc zooms the camera. The phone's digital zoom pushes in, the lighthouse growing in the viewfinder from a white speck to a recognizable tower. The image is grainier than his other captures — digital zoom does that — but the lighthouse fills enough of the frame that its features are clear: the cylindrical body, the red cap, the balcony railing, the rocks at its base.

Tap.

Blue flash. The deep chime again. The card rises, pulsing blue.

**Rare 🔵.**

*"Oa! Lại từ hiếm nữa!"*
("Wow! Another rare word!")

**ngọn hải đăng** — the Vietnamese label. Three syllables, a phrase most Vietnamese children have heard in stories but never spoken in English.

*"Đây là ngọn hải đăng. Tiếng Anh là:"*
("This is a lighthouse. In English:")

**"Lighthouse."** `/ˈlaɪthaʊs/`

Rare 🔵 · **50 pts.** Another blue-bordered card. Another fifty points. Two Rare captures in three minutes, both from a rest stop on the coastal road.

The "Say it!" prompt appears. Phúc presses PTT. "Lighthouse!" — the compound word comes out clean, the two halves intuitive: light + house. ASR: `"lighthouse"`. Match. ⭐⭐⭐.

The counter at the top of the screen has climbed dramatically. Two Rares at 50 each, plus the Uncommons and Commons from the highway — the car ride is producing points at a rate Phúc has never achieved in a single session.

> 🗺️ **Flow mapping** — This is §3.1 Vision handling a distance shot with digital zoom. The lighthouse is approximately 300 meters away. The photograph, even zoomed, is lower resolution than a close-up capture. But the AI does not require pixel-perfect images — it requires recognizable features. A lighthouse has one of the most distinctive silhouettes in the built environment: tall, cylindrical, topped with a lantern room. Modern multimodal LLMs identify lighthouses from paintings, sketches, low-resolution photographs, and partially obscured angles. Phúc's zoomed photo clears the 0.7 confidence threshold (0.82 in this case) because the shape is unmistakable.

Mom calls from the drink stall. "Phúc! Nước dừa!" ("Phúc! Coconut water!") He jogs over, phone still in hand, and accepts a plastic cup of fresh coconut water with ice. He drinks it standing up, facing the sea, the lighthouse in the distance, his Collection Book two Rare captures richer than it was five minutes ago.

Thi returns from the restroom and sees Phúc grinning. "What did you catch?" she asks, leaning over to look at his phone. He shows her the coconut palm card with its blue border and the lighthouse card beside it. She raises her eyebrows.

"Không tệ đâu," she says. ("Not bad.")

From a thirteen-year-old sister, that is a standing ovation.

> 🔊 **Audio-first moment** — Throughout the rest stop, Phúc's captures followed the same audio-first pattern as the in-car captures: TTS auto-plays the full scaffolding sequence on every card reveal. But here, outside the car, the audio serves a different purpose. In the car, audio compensated for split attention between window and screen. At the rest stop, audio shares the moment with bystanders — Thi hears "coconut palm" spoken aloud and leans in to look. Mom hears the Rare chime from the drink stall and smiles. The audio-first design creates ambient awareness: people near the child know something is happening, and the sounds are pleasant enough — chimes, cheerful mascot voice, English words — that they attract rather than repel attention.

> 💡 **Design principle — Transit time is learning time.** The session highlight is not any single capture. It is the total: twelve words across five categories, including two Rares, earned during a car ride that would otherwise have been two hours of staring at a headrest. The changing landscape — city streets → highway → coast — naturally diversified the vocabulary without any mission prompt or curriculum guidance. The boring back seat became a safari vehicle. SnapLearn did not need a "car mode" or a "travel feature." The collection loop, the rarity engine, and the audio-first interface were sufficient. The road did the rest.
